Baird Medical · Nasdaq: BDMD · Guangzhou, China

Microwave ablation for thyroid nodules

Baird Medical describes itself as a specialized healthcare innovator dedicated exclusively to thyroid related diseases. It makes single-use microwave ablation needles and the therapeutic apparatus they are used with. Through 2024 it sold almost entirely to customers in mainland China; it now also sells in Hong Kong and the United States, and in 2026 it began licensing the technology outside China. 20-F, Apr 24, 2026 6-K, Sep 30, 2026

The business

Needles, the apparatus they are used with, and the hospitals that buy them

Microwave ablation, in the 20-F’s description, is a minimally invasive technique that uses heat generated by microwave energy to denature and coagulate the protein of tumor cells. Baird’s needles are single-use consumables used together with its therapeutic apparatus. 20-F, Item 4.B

Product · Consumable

Microwave ablation needles

Fine and long single-use needles, used with the apparatus for each treatment. The company says it has sold approximately 100,000 of them. 20-F

An annual licence to use the technology outside China

The company grants licences to third parties in specified territories outside the PRC, including Europe, Brazil and the UK. Disclosures

What the filings say about risk

Eight matters a reader should have in front of them. Each is in the company’s own filings, and each carries its source. Figures for the six months ended Jun 30, 2026 and balances at Jun 30, 2026 are unaudited; 2025 means the year ended Dec 31, 2025.

01

Going concern: management’s assessment

“management concluded that there was no substantial doubt about the Company’s ability to continue as a going concern within one year after the issuance date of the financial statements.”

The 20-F/A bases that on cash and restricted cash, positive working capital ($22.6M at Dec 31, 2025), the non-cash nature of much of the 2025 loss, the improvement in operating cash outflows, historical renewal of its short-term bank facilities, continuing financial support from Haimei Wu, a non-demand confirmation from Betters Medical Investment Holdings Limited, subsequent collections and expected 2026 profitability drivers. Ms. Wu is able to provide US$2.0 million, supported by properties she owns. Betters Medical confirmed that what the company owes it ($4.74M at Dec 31, 2025) will not be required to be repaid within 12 months. The company adds that there can be no assurance its actual cash requirements will not exceed management’s current expectations. At Dec 31, 2025 all of the $42.5M of net receivables was classified as current, and the 20-F/A expects about $26.7M of what remained uncollected to be collected in 2027.

The unaudited first-half statements reach the same conclusion at Jun 30, 2026, when cash was $138,551, and say the ability to continue “is dependent on management’s ability to successfully execute its business plan which includes reducing the fixed labor cost, pursuing cooperation opportunities and potential financing.” 20-F/A, Item 5 6-K, Sep 30, 2026, going concern assessment

02

Receivables are large and old

At Dec 31, 2025 net receivables were $42.5M, of which the company says $39.7M was overdue against terms of 30 to 180 days. At Jun 30, 2026 gross receivables were $50.2M and $31.8M was more than one year past its original terms, up from $19.9M and $8.3M. The allowance was $5.8M, with $0.90M added in the half. $13.7M is now classified as non-current, and some customers and the licensees have credit terms of up to 365 days. 20-F/A 6-K, Note 3

03

A few customers and licensees carry the revenue

One deliverer group, subsidiaries of a listed distributor of medical devices and pharmaceutical products in the PRC, was 24.0% of receivables at Jun 30, 2026. Licensing, from unnamed licensees, was 48% of H1 2026 revenue. Two vendors supplied 34.3% and 26.4% of H1 2026 purchases of inventories and equipment. 6-K, Note 16 20-F, Item 4.B

04

Financing and dilution

On Sep 24, 2026 the company agreed a senior 8% original issue discount convertible note of $4,347,826 with a 12-month term. No interest accrues unless there is an event of default, when it is 15% a year. The conversion price is the lower of $0.9842 and 92% of the lowest daily VWAP in the seven trading days before the conversion notice (85% on a default), never below a floor of 20% of the Nasdaq minimum price (if the price would fall below the floor, the company either lowers the floor or pays the difference in cash or adds it to the principal), and the holder is capped at 9.99% of the shares. The company issued 110,070 shares at $0.79, the prior day’s closing price, as an additional discount. About US$4 million was received, which the prospectus estimates at US$3.7 million after fees; the filing gives the date as both September 8 and September 28. 6-K, Sep 30, 2026, first-half statements 424B5, Sep 24, 2026

Subject to four conditions, the purchaser has the obligation to lend a further $3,680,000 within 180 days of the closing date. The conditions are that the prior day’s closing price is more than $1.00, the conversion shares can be sold under an effective F-3, less than $300,000 of principal is outstanding, and the other conditions in the purchase agreement are met. The closing price was $0.7901 on Sep 22, 2026. Up to $6 million more may be lent in two closings within 360 days by mutual consent. 6-K, Sep 30, 2026, note agreement 424B5, Sep 24, 2026

The note is senior and secured. The company has pledged all of the equity of Baird Medical LLC, and the company and Baird Medical LLC have granted a lien on substantially all of their assets located in the United States, including intellectual property. The company and its subsidiaries, other than one excluded subsidiary, also guarantee the note. The prospectus supplement says that at the floor price up to 27,517,886 shares could be issued on conversion, against 32,002,370 outstanding, which would bring the total to up to 59,630,326 shares, and that the fixed conversion price is reduced to the price of any later issuance made below it. 6-K, Sep 30, 2026, note agreement 424B5, Sep 24, 2026

Other claims on the share count: 11,500,000 shares issuable on outstanding warrants; 290,000 Series A preferred shares, which the 20-F treats as convertible into 290,000 ordinary shares; a $2.0 million subscription by Wu Wenyuan for 200,000 more preferred shares that had not closed at the date of the 20-F; and earnout shares, 8,823,529 held by Betters Medical (eight years) and 1,350,000 by the SPAC sponsor (five years), which vest only if the VWAP reaches $12.50 for 20 of 30 trading days or on a qualifying change of control. At Jun 30, 2026 there were 42,208,763 shares issued and 32,002,370 outstanding. In 2025 it issued 4,563,745 shares as share-based compensation to individuals and 633,529 as share-based payment to third-party companies. 6-K, Sep 30, 2026 424B5, Sep 24, 2026 6-K, Sep 30, 2026, first-half statements 20-F

06

Listing, auditor and inspection

The 20-F says that if the PCAOB cannot inspect or investigate completely auditors located in China and the company does not retain an auditor it can inspect, its shares would be delisted from Nasdaq and could not trade over the counter in the United States under the HFCAA. On Feb 10, 2026 the company dismissed Kreit & Chiu CPA LLP and appointed Guangdong Prouden CPAs GP for the year ended Dec 31, 2025. The 6-K reports no disagreements, and that the only reportable event was the material weaknesses below.

The 20-F for the year ended Dec 31, 2025 was amended on Jul 27, 2026 after SEC staff comments, and the 20-F for the year ended Dec 31, 2024 was filed after a notice of late filing on Apr 30, 2025. None of the filings reviewed for this page reports a Nasdaq listing-standards deficiency notice. The prospectus supplement gives a closing price of $0.7901 on Sep 22, 2026 and has a risk factor on Nasdaq’s new $5 million minimum market value rule, which the SEC approved on Jul 22, 2026 and which was stayed on Jul 29, 2026. 07

Controls, a licence lapse and uninsured risks

The chief executive and the acting chief financial officer, Jie Li, concluded that disclosure controls were not effective at Dec 31, 2025, because of two material weaknesses: too few accounting staff with U.S. GAAP and SEC reporting knowledge, and no comprehensive accounting policies and procedures manual. The 20-F says management concluded the financial statements nonetheless fairly present the company’s results. It names the affected areas as certain equity transactions, sales returns, leases and the expense of prepayments for research and development costs; prepaid research and development was $11.8M at Dec 31, 2025. 20-F, Item 15 20-F/A, Item 5

The company let a manufacturing licence lapse in 2021, leaving about two months without one. In November 2024 it was subject to a confiscation of about RMB4.2 million of sales revenue and an administrative penalty of about RMB434,000; the related cash freeze was lifted in January 2026. It has chosen not to carry litigation, product liability or business interruption insurance. Unpaid social insurance and housing fund contributions stood at $0.3M, $0.4M and $0.8M at the end of 2023, 2024 and 2025. 20-F, Items 3 and 15

08

Operating in China

In 2023, 2024 and 2025 substantially all revenue was derived from the PRC, which the 20-F defines to include Hong Kong, and the renminbi is not freely convertible. The company concluded that the restricted net assets of its subsidiaries exceed 25% of consolidated net assets, so it presents condensed parent-company statements. The 20-F says all raw materials were procured in China in 2023, 2024 and 2025, and that two suppliers each exceeded 10% of cost of revenues in 2025. The prospectus supplement puts the two largest suppliers at 46.0% and 29.6% of that cost. 6-K, Notes 16 and 18 20-F 424B5, Sep 24, 2026
